# Journal of Business Ethics -- Style & Fit Dossier

## 1. Snapshot

The **Journal of Business Ethics (JBE)** is a flagship Springer title (journal number 10551), one of the most prominent and highly cited venues in the business-ethics and responsible-management fields. Its self-described mission, taken verbatim from the aims and scope page, is to "improve the human condition by promoting ethical discussion and debate in business" (https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/aims-and-scope). The journal reads "business" expansively -- as "all systems involved in the exchange of goods and services" -- and "ethics" equally broadly, as covering "all human action aimed at securing a good life." The domains it analyzes "from a moral viewpoint" include systems of production, consumption, marketing, advertising, social and economic accounting, labour relations, public relations, and organisational behaviour. The editorial premium, stated repeatedly across the journal's editorial materials, is on contributing "something new or unique to the discourse" of business ethics.

Crucially for any prospective author, JBE publishes **both normative/philosophical scholarship and empirical research**. The aims and scope explicitly states that "both speculative philosophy as well as reports of empirical research are welcomed," and that "empirical manuscripts in any quantitative or qualitative methodological tradition may summarize studies that are cross-sectional or longitudinal, single- or multi-cultural, single- or multi-industry, and societal- or industry/firm level." In other words, the journal accommodates conceptual papers, quantitative empirical papers, qualitative empirical papers, and literature reviews. Authors must self-identify their manuscript as an "original paper (conceptual papers, literature reviews, or empirical papers)" at submission, and must additionally choose one of the journal's **30-plus specialized Sections**, each with its own Section Editor (e.g., "Corporate Responsibility: Quantitative Issues," "Corporate Responsibility: Theoretical/Qualitative Issues," "Leadership and Ethics: Quantitative Analysis," "Philosophy and Business Ethics") (https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/updates/17211284).

The disciplinary center of gravity is **management, organizational behavior, applied social psychology, and moral philosophy/business ethics** -- not computer science, not HCI, and not economics. The typical audience is business-school faculty, management and organization-studies scholars, and ethicists. A non-negotiable editorial filter follows from this: empirical papers must "explain the ethics" of the phenomena they study, and normative papers must "embed their analysis in a thick understanding of the contexts" (see the editorial "Contributing to Business Ethics through Thick Conceptualization," https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-024-05896-5). A paper that is method-only, or that treats its phenomenon purely as a technical or statistical object without an explicit moral/ethics contribution, is off-mission regardless of its empirical quality.

## 2. Style guide / author guidelines

**Template / format.** JBE accepts manuscripts in either **Word (.docx) or LaTeX**. The journal is emphatic that editable source files are mandatory: "Please ensure you provide all relevant editable source files at every submission and revision. Failing to submit a complete set of editable source files will result in your article not being considered for review" (https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/submission-guidelines). PDF-only submissions are not accepted for review. For LaTeX, Springer journals use the unified **Springer Nature LaTeX template, document class `sn-jnl`** (the `sn-jnl.cls` class), invoked as `\documentclass[<options>]{sn-jnl}`. For a single-column social-sciences layout the appropriate reference-style option is `sn-basic`, i.e. `\documentclass[sn-basic]{sn-jnl}`. The class offers selectable bibliography styles -- `sn-basic`, `sn-apa`, `sn-chicago`, `sn-vancouver-num`, `sn-mathphys-num`, `sn-nature`, and others -- and for JBE the author-date families (`sn-basic` or `sn-apa`) are the correct choices (sources: https://thelatexlab.com/blog/springer-latex-template-formatting/; https://github.com/godkingjay/springer-nature-latex-template/blob/master/sn-jnl.cls; https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/springer-nature-latex-template/myxmhdsbzkyd). For review, manuscripts are prepared **single-column, double-spaced, with continuous line numbering**; the two-column layout is for typeset proofs only. Word submissions do not require a specific Springer template; a well-structured, well-written .docx is accepted directly.

**Double-anonymous review.** JBE "adopts a double anonymous review policy, where authors' and reviewers' identity is not disclosed to each other." This drives several concrete style rules. All author-identifying information **and acknowledgements** go on a **separate title page** not sent to reviewers: "The Journal of Business Ethics requests that acknowledgements be included in the title page of the submission (which includes the author's information and is not sent to reviewers) to ensure that the work is fully anonymized." Self-citation must be disguised: "If authors must refer to their own work, this should be done in the third person, avoiding any self-identifying statements." If reviewers are shown data, "it is strongly suggested that authorship in the data is also anonymised" (all from https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/submission-guidelines).

**Citation style.** JBE uses an **author-date** system -- the Springer Basic / SPBASIC family (CSL `springer-basic-author-date`; LaTeX `spbasic.bst` / `sn-basic`), which Springer describes as "based on Harvard style and the recommendations of the Council of Biology Editors." It is **NOT numeric and NOT ACM Reference Format.** Examination of recent published JBE articles confirms the in-text form renders in the **APA-like comma convention**: `(Author, Year)` parenthetically and `Author (Year)` in narrative position; two authors joined by "and"; three or more authors abbreviated to "et al." Page numbers follow the year where needed. (The SPBASIC *machine default* technically omits the comma -- `(Author Year)` -- but JBE's typeset house style shows the comma, which resolves the earlier SPBASIC-vs-APA ambiguity. The exact comma form for any given submission is best confirmed against a current issue, but the published evidence is consistent: **comma form**.) The reference list is **alphabetical by first-author surname**, authors listed surname-first then initials, with the Springer Nature rule to "include all authors unless there are six or more, in which case only the first author should be given, followed by 'et al.'" Article titles are roman, sentence case, ending in a full stop; journal names are italicized. Pattern: `Surname Initials (Year) Article title. Journal Name Volume:pages.` (e.g., `Petit C, Sieffermann J (2007) Testing consumer preferences for iced-coffee... 18:161-172.`). **DOIs are expected** on journal-article references to support automated citation linking; Springer supplies EndNote and BibTeX outputs for the purpose.

**Abstract.** Default limit is **250 words**, extendable: "The abstract length can be increased from the 250-word limit to up to 450 words if the topic dictates, and to allow full compliance with the relevant reporting guidelines" (https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/submission-guidelines). The standard format is a **single, unstructured narrative paragraph** (no sub-headings); structured abstracts are not generally required (whether any specific Section mandates a structured abstract is **UNCONFIRMED**). Springer's general guidance is that the abstract "should not contain any undefined abbreviations or unspecified references" -- i.e., **no citations in the abstract** (the precise JBE-page wording of the no-citations rule was not captured verbatim, so it is **partially UNCONFIRMED**, but it is uniformly observed in published JBE abstracts).

**Structure expectations.** For empirical papers the norm is IMRaD-style organization extended with management-field conventions: Introduction -> Theory/Literature & Hypothesis Development -> Method -> Results -> **Discussion** -> **Implications** (theoretical AND practical/managerial) -> Limitations/Future Research -> Conclusion. A **dedicated theory / conceptual-development section is strongly expected**; the journal's stated value is that papers contribute "something new or unique," and empirical papers must "explain the ethics" of their phenomena. A separate **Discussion** plus explicit **Implications** is a firm disciplinary norm in this venue. **Numbered hypotheses (H1/H2...) are common and expected for quantitative empirical papers** (the management/OB convention) but are **not a hard journal-wide requirement** -- qualitative and normative papers do not use them (the formal-requirement status of numbered hypotheses is **UNCONFIRMED**; treat it as a strong convention, not a rule). JBE does **not** impose a single rigid template because it spans normative, quantitative, and qualitative traditions; the expected skeleton depends on the chosen Section and paper type.

**Figures and tables.** Numbered with **Arabic numerals**, cited "in consecutive numerical order" in the text. **Captions/legends go in the manuscript text file, not embedded in the figure files** ("captions should be included in the text file of the manuscript, not in the figure file"). Previously published material must be credited at the end of the table caption. Table footnotes use superscript lower-case letters; asterisks denote significance. Springer artwork standards apply: EPS for vector graphics, TIFF at >=300 dpi for halftones, >=1200 dpi for line art, >=600 dpi for combination art; color is free for online publication and submitted as RGB (8 bits/channel). (Note convention contrast with the user's paper: JBE places **table titles above tables** and figure captions are supplied in text -- but note Springer's strict rule that **AI-generated images cannot be included in publications**, directly relevant to an LLM paper.)

**Keywords.** "Please provide **4 to 6 keywords** which can be used for indexing purposes" (https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/submission-guidelines).

**Length.** "The average length of an article for all sections other than the book review section is approximately **8,000-10,000 words (including references)**" (same source). This is a norm, not a hard cap. Supplementary Information is supported and supplied electronically, with SI figures numbered separately; appendices/online appendices are permitted (exact SI size limits are **UNCONFIRMED**).

**Required statements / declarations.** Springer requires a consolidated **"Declarations"** block placed **before the reference list**, summarized also on the separate title page. Components: **Funding**; **Competing interests / Conflict of interest** (mandatory; disclose interests "within the last 3 years of beginning the work"); **Ethics approval / IRB** ("study-specific approval by the appropriate ethics committee for research involving humans and/or animals," with the corresponding author "prepared to collect documentation of compliance"); **Consent** (informed consent and consent to publish where relevant); **Welfare of animals** (where applicable); **Data, Materials and/or Code availability** (a **data availability statement is required**, supporting reproducibility); and **Authors' contributions**. On **AI use**, Springer Nature policy applies: "Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy Springer authorship criteria" (LLMs cannot be authors); "Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the **Methods section** (or a suitable alternative part) of the manuscript"; pure AI-assisted copy-editing "does not need to be declared"; and "Images generated using AI cannot be included in publications" (https://www.springer.com/gp/editorial-policies/artificial-intelligence--ai-/25428500). On **positionality/reflexivity**: there is **no journal-wide formal requirement** -- it is encouraged as good practice for interpretive/qualitative work only (**UNCONFIRMED as a formal requirement; treat as not required**).

## 3. Published-paper exemplars

A meta-finding first, because it is load-bearing for fit: **JBE does publish AI/algorithmic-bias work, but the profile skews toward (a) behavioral/experimental studies of human perceptions of algorithms and (b) normative/conceptual essays on AI ethics. Direct LLM/GPT output-audit studies -- probing a model's generations for bias -- appear RARE-to-ABSENT in JBE as of mid-2026.** The genuine "LLM" papers JBE has run are conceptual/normative, not computational audits. The closest empirical analogues to a GPT gender-bias audit are vignette/survey experiments on *humans*, not model-output audits. This is itself a fit signal: to land in JBE, a model-audit paper must foreground ethics framing and organizational/societal implications rather than the computational method.

**Paper 1 -- "Biased Humans, (Un)Biased Algorithms?" (the most topic-apt comparator).** Florian Pethig & Julia Kroenung, 2023, *Journal of Business Ethics* 183(3): 637-652. DOI 10.1007/s10551-022-05071-8 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-022-05071-8; OA PDF: https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/61378/1/s10551-022-05071-8.pdf). This is the single closest topical match in JBE to a gender-bias study -- it asks whether women prefer algorithmic over human evaluation when they fear gender disadvantage, introducing the construct "relative algorithmic objectivity." It is a **quantitative multi-study experimental paper** (three studies, total N = 1,107): an incentive-compatible online experiment plus scenario/vignette experiments, with group comparisons and mediation analysis (PROCESS-style; exact tests UNCONFIRMED). **Numbered hypotheses are present**, with a dedicated **Theoretical Background & Hypothesis Development** section grounded in social-psychology constructs (stigma, stereotype threat, objectivity). Structure (inferred from norms + snippets): Introduction -> Theory & Hypotheses -> Study 1/2/3 each with Method/Results -> General Discussion (Theoretical Implications, Practical Implications, Limitations & Future Research) -> Conclusion -> Declarations -> References. The abstract is a single ~150-200-word narrative paragraph with no citations, opening "Across three studies (N = 1107)..."; **4 keywords** (Algorithms; Gender bias; Stigma; Objectivity); citation style author-date APA-like with comma; results tables plus a mediation/model figure; ~16 pages. Contribution type: empirical finding + theoretical mechanism, with an explicit ethics framing ("ethical management of algorithms").

**Paper 2 -- "Applicants' Fairness Perceptions of Algorithm-Driven Hiring Procedures."** Maude Lavanchy, Patrick Reichert, Jayanth Narayanan & Krishna Savani, 2023, *Journal of Business Ethics* 188(1): 125-150. DOI 10.1007/s10551-022-05320-w (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-022-05320-w; OA PDF: https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/99045/1/Savani_Applicants_Fairness_Perceptions.pdf). A bias-adjacent AI/fairness paper on perceived procedural fairness of hiring algorithms. **Quantitative, four MTurk studies**, with a priori power analyses (ANOVA, medium effect, 80% power, alpha = .05), a procedural-justice DV, and mediation of a "uniqueness-neglect" mechanism. **Numbered hypotheses present**; dedicated organizational-justice theory section. Structure (inferred): Introduction -> Theory & Hypotheses -> Studies 1-4 each with Method/Participants/Design/Measures/Results -> General Discussion (Theoretical Contributions, Practical/Managerial Implications, Limitations, Future Research) -> Conclusion -> Declarations -> References, with stimuli appendices. Abstract is a single ~180-230-word narrative paragraph, "Across four studies..."; author-date APA-like citations with DOIs; several results tables plus likely a design/mediation figure; ~26 pages (longer end, reflecting four studies). Tone: formal empirical OB/management with strong managerial-implications orientation and a fairness/justice ethics frame.

**Paper 3 -- "Artificial Aesthetics and Ethical Ambiguity: Exploring Business Ethics in the Context of AI-driven Creativity" (recent, qualitative, genuine GenAI topic).** Cheng Xu, Yanqi Sun & Haibo Zhou, 2025 (online Oct 2024), *Journal of Business Ethics* 199(4): 671-692. DOI 10.1007/s10551-024-05837-2 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-024-05837-2). A generative-AI empirical paper showing JBE's **qualitative/interpretive** house style -- a useful contrast to the experimental papers and to a quantitative audit. **Mixed-methods, qualitatively dominant**: a six-month collaboration with 34 artists using AI tools, with interviews, observation, and diary analysis, analyzed via inductive/interpretive coding (grounded-theory-style; exact scheme UNCONFIRMED). **No numbered hypotheses**; results presented as themes with illustrative quotes; an explicit **Posthumanist** theoretical framework woven through. New construct: "aesthetic loss of control." Structure (inferred): Introduction -> Theoretical Framework -> Methods -> Findings (thematic, with quotes) -> Discussion -> Implications -> Limitations -> Conclusion -> Declarations -> References. Abstract a single ~200-250-word narrative paragraph; author-date APA-like citations; likely a Gioia-style data-structure table and a conceptual-framework figure (note Springer's no-AI-images rule); ~22 pages; reflexive, theory-rich, interpretive tone.

**Paper 4 -- "Generative Artificial Intelligence as Hypercommons: Ethics of Authorship and Ownership" (recent, normative, by the Editors-in-Chief).** Gazi Islam & Michelle Greenwood, 2024, *Journal of Business Ethics* 192(4): 659-663. DOI 10.1007/s10551-024-05741-9 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-024-05741-9; OA PDF: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10551-024-05741-9.pdf). A genuine ChatGPT-era GenAI piece and an editorial essay by the journal's own EiCs -- an authoritative window into JBE's preferred normative voice on AI (coining "hypercommons" to describe the invisible collective labor behind LLMs). **Pure conceptual/normative argument**: no data, no method, no hypotheses; concepts (commons, hypercommons, moral agency, authorship) developed discursively under thematic headings rather than IMRaD. Short ~120-160-word narrative abstract; author-date APA-like citations; no figures/tables; ~5 pages (an editorial-essay length, NOT a model for a full research article's ~8,000-10,000 words). Tone: argumentative, philosophically engaged, first-person-plural editorial voice.

Cross-cutting, in practice: citation style is uniformly **author-date, APA-like with the comma** (not numeric/ACM), reference lists alphabetical with DOIs; empirical papers are overwhelmingly **multi-study, IMRaD, theory-and-hypothesis-driven, with a General Discussion that always splits Theoretical Implications + Practical/Managerial Implications + Limitations**; abstracts are single narrative paragraphs without citations; the disciplinary register is management/OB/social-psychology for empirics and ethics-philosophy/critical-management for conceptual work -- **never CS or HCI**.

## 4. How it differs from YOUR paper

The user's paper ("Reversal and Persistence of Gender Biases in GPT Models," Bopp & Pinho Neto) is an applied-labor-economics + algorithmic-fairness audit, currently styled for *Public Choice* on the conservative Motoki et al. (2024) template: an economist's voice, generic LaTeX article class, natbib/apalike author-date, ~80% empirical, OLS linear-probability models with robust SEs read as percentage points, organized Intro -> Lit Review -> Methodology -> Results (Test 1/2/3) -> Conclusion, with no Discussion section, no numbered hypotheses, and no theory-building section. In prose: the single deepest gap is **disciplinary framing and contribution type.** The user's paper is a clean econometric audit -- gender bias is an outcome variable, the contribution is a measured coefficient pattern (overcorrection in aligned chat models; persistent occupational stereotypes). JBE will not accept that as a contribution on its own terms; it demands that the paper "explain the ethics" and surface theoretical and **practical/managerial implications**. The second gap is **structure**: JBE empirical papers are theory-first and Discussion-heavy, whereas the user's paper has neither a theory-building section nor a Discussion. Adding a genuine conceptual/theory-development section (e.g., grounding the audit in stereotyping theory, algorithmic-fairness ethics, or organizational-justice constructs) and a full Discussion + Implications section is mandatory, not cosmetic. The third gap is **method register**: JBE's quantitative AI-ethics papers are behavioral experiments on humans (vignettes, MTurk, mediation), not model-output audits with OLS linear-probability models. The audit method is not disqualifying, but it is atypical and must be wrapped in an ethics-and-implications narrative to read as in-register. The smaller, mechanical gaps -- citation comma form (already author-date), abstract to a single <=250-word paragraph, `sn-jnl`/`sn-apa` template, double-anonymous title-page handling, Declarations block with data/code availability and an LLM-use note in Methods, moving the 20+-regression appendix to SI -- are straightforward.

## 5. Fit & recommendation

**Fit as-is: poor.** The topic is in scope (algorithmic gender bias is squarely within JBE's interest in fairness, discrimination, and the ethics of AI in business), but the paper's *form and contribution type* are not. JBE is a business-ethics/management journal; it publishes AI-bias work as either behavioral perception experiments or normative essays, and -- importantly -- **direct LLM output-audit studies appear rare-to-absent in the journal as of mid-2026.** Submitted in its current econometric-audit form, with no theory section, no Discussion/Implications, no explicit ethics contribution, and a method register foreign to the venue, the paper would very likely be desk-rejected or bounced as "out of scope / no ethics contribution."

**Specific changes required to make it viable:**
1. **Reframe the contribution around ethics and organizational/societal implications.** The measured "overcorrection" and persistent occupational stereotypes must become an *ethics* argument: what does it mean morally and managerially that aligned LLMs over-correct on gender, and that hiring/HR/marketing systems built on them inherit residual stereotypes? Add a substantial **Implications** treatment (theoretical + practical/managerial).
2. **Add a dedicated Theory / Conceptual-Development section** grounding the audit in business-ethics constructs (stereotyping, algorithmic fairness ethics, organizational justice, responsibility/governance), and consider stating **numbered hypotheses** to match the quantitative-OB convention.
3. **Restructure to JBE IMRaD-plus:** Introduction -> Theory & Hypotheses -> Method -> Results -> **General Discussion** -> **Implications (theoretical + managerial)** -> Limitations/Future Research -> Conclusion -> Declarations -> References.
4. **Convert the format**: `sn-jnl` (`sn-apa`/`sn-basic`) or Word; single-column, double-spaced, line-numbered; double-anonymous with a separate title page; abstract to one paragraph <=250 words, no citations; **4-6 keywords**; Declarations block with **data/code availability**, competing interests (3-yr window), funding, author contributions, and an **LLM-use statement in the Methods section** (LLMs not authors; no AI-generated images). Trim to ~8,000-10,000 words and push the 20+-regression appendix to electronic SI.
5. **Consider pairing the audit with a perception or normative layer** -- the surest way to look in-register for JBE would be to complement the computational audit with a human-perception study or a normative analysis of the ethics of LLM gender behavior in business contexts.

**Adaptation effort rating: HIGH.** The mechanical conversions (template, citations, abstract, declarations) are easy, but the substantive demands -- a new theory section, a full Discussion + Implications, an explicit ethics contribution, and a method/voice re-register from economist-audit to management-ethics -- amount to a near-rewrite of the paper's framing and roughly a third of its prose. This is not a reformatting job; it is a re-genre-ing job.

**The single most important reframing the journal would demand:** stop presenting gender bias as an econometric outcome variable and start presenting it as a *business-ethics problem* -- the paper must "explain the ethics" of LLM gender behavior and deliver concrete theoretical and managerial/societal implications, with the regression audit demoted to evidence in service of an ethics argument rather than the contribution itself.

**Candor on scope and method:** the *topic* is genuinely in scope; the *method* (an LLM output audit with OLS linear-probability models) is atypical-to-unprecedented for JBE and, on its own, unlikely to satisfy the journal's ethics-contribution bar. Realistically, JBE is a stretch target for this paper without the substantive reframing above; the user's current *Public Choice* target is a far more natural home for the econometric-audit form.
