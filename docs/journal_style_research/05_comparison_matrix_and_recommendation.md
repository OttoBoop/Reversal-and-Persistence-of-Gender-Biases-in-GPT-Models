# Cross-Journal Style Comparison & Fit Recommendation

> Synthesis across four candidate venues — **AI & Society** (Springer), **JAIR** (AI Access Foundation), **PACMHCI** (ACM), and **Journal of Business Ethics** (Springer) — benchmarked against the current **Public Choice** baseline for *"Reversal and Persistence of Gender Biases in GPT Models"* (Bopp & Pinho Neto). This document distills the four per-journal dossiers (`01`–`04`) into a single comparison matrix, an overall fit ranking, and a concrete conversion checklist for the top pick.

---

## 1. The baseline in one paragraph

The user's paper is an **applied-labor-economics + algorithmic-fairness** manuscript written in an economist's voice — squarely in the Bertrand–Mullainathan resume-audit tradition, with a secondary NLP/ML framing — currently targeted at **Public Choice** (Springer) on the conservative Motoki et al. (2024) "arroz com feijão" template. Gender bias is treated not as a social-theory construct or a fairness metric but as a **measured outcome variable**: the dependent variable is P(male character), estimated with an **OLS linear-probability model with heteroskedasticity-consistent (robust) standard errors**, reporting p-values, R², and coefficients read as percentage points (e.g. β₁ = −0.61 means −61 pp). It is roughly **80% empirical** — 62,000+ generated stories across 14+ GPT models in 3 prompt-based experiments — and reproducibility-heavy (Jupyter notebook, CSVs, 896 numeric audit checks). Format is a **generic LaTeX `article` class** (12pt, a4paper, no journal template) with **natbib/apalike author-date** citations (`citet`/`citep`, ~80–100 refs, no numeric citations); a **~240-word, two-paragraph, unstructured narrative+quantitative abstract** with no formal hypotheses; **5 keywords**; and the structure Introduction → Literature Review (4 subsecs) → Methodology (8 subsecs, econometric equations in-text) → Results (Test 1/2/3) → Conclusion — **with no separate Discussion section, no numbered hypotheses (H1/H2), and no dedicated theory-building section**. Tone is formal first-person-plural, hedged, policy-aware, conservative; ~15 main pages plus a large appendix (20+ regressions, 5 profession cross-tabs); figure captions below figures, table captions above tables. The headline finding is **"overcorrection"** — newer aligned chat models (GPT-3.5+) swing to a pro-female association (β ≈ −0.61) while older completion GPT-3 models were near-neutral (β ≈ −0.03), and occupational stereotypes persist (all receptionists female, 98% blue-collar male).

## 3. Overall fit ranking

**1 — AI & Society (best fit).** This is the only one of the four where topical scope fit and stylistic adaptation cost are *both* favorable. LLM/GPT gender-bias and algorithmic-fairness audits are actively and currently published here (the dossier's Exemplar 1, "ChatGPT is a gender bias echo-chamber in HR recruitment," is a near-twin: real NLP rigor wrapped in a social-ethical argument, co-billing a named framework). On the mechanical axis the gap is the smallest of any venue: the citation habit is already author-date (a light restyle to Springer Basic, not a rethink), the abstract needs only to collapse to one paragraph, the keyword count already complies (5, within 4–6), figure/table caption placement *already matches*, and there is length headroom (~10,000 words vs. the current ~15 pp). The genuinely new work is substantive but bounded and requires **no new data**: add a theory/framing section, add a Discussion+Implications section, translate the econometrics for a non-economist reader, elevate a named framework contribution, and clear the production gates (double-blind anonymization + the Statements-and-Declarations block). Net of topic-in-scope plus the lowest adaptation cost, AI & Society wins decisively.

**2 — Journal of Business Ethics.** Topically in scope (algorithmic gender bias sits squarely within JBE's fairness/discrimination/AI-ethics interests), and the citation style is already author-date (APA-like with comma) so the mechanical conversion is easy. It ranks second rather than first because the *contribution type* gap is large and the *method register* is foreign: direct LLM output-audit studies are rare-to-absent at JBE, whose quantitative AI-ethics papers are behavioral experiments on humans (vignette/MTurk + mediation), and the journal will not accept a measured coefficient pattern as a contribution on its own — it demands the paper "explain the ethics" and deliver theoretical + managerial/societal implications. That means a near-rewrite (new theory section, full Discussion+Implications, an explicit ethics re-pitch, likely numbered hypotheses), pushing effort to HIGH. It edges out the two CS/HCI venues because it values author-date citation and quantitative empirical work, and because its ethics-and-implications demand is a re-framing of existing material rather than a re-platforming into an alien epistemology.

**3 — PACMHCI.** The topic is adjacent to the AI-ethics work PACMHCI reads, but the fit is poor on both method and framing, and the journal applies an explicit scope test (contributions must center the *social aspects of technology mediation*, grounded in CSCW concepts/theory) that can trigger desk-level scope rejection of a stand-alone econometric audit. Pure GPT bias audits of exactly this genre systematically land at FAccT/EAAMO/AIES/CHI, not PACMHCI. Conversion is HIGH effort and amounts to "re-genre-ing": re-platform to `acmart`, switch to numeric `[n]` citations (a direction change from author-date), add CSCW theory grounding and a Discussion+Implications section, demote the econometrics, and ideally add a qualitative/interpretive strand. It ranks above JAIR only because the *abstract, no-hypotheses, long-with-appendix shape, and one caption convention* already align, and because AI-ethics topics are at least within its reading sphere; it ranks below JBE because the epistemology (human-as-subject, interpretation-as-evidence) is the opposite of the user's (model-as-object, regression-as-evidence) and because the citation direction reverses.

**4 — JAIR (worst fit).** Although the topic is nominally in scope (a Fairness & Bias in AI Special Track exists) and several surface conventions already align (author-date citations, equation-in-text methodology, a large reproducibility appendix that JAIR's online-appendix norm actively welcomes), the *genre* has essentially **zero published precedent**: empirical prompt-based audits of commercial LLMs do not appear in JAIR, which publishes measurement-theory, formal-fairness, and survey contributions evaluated by AI researchers looking for a method or measurement contribution. Making the paper fit requires recasting an OLS-driven product audit as a measurement-methodology, formal-fairness, or survey-with-recommendations contribution — a rewrite of the paper's intellectual frame, not a reformat — for HIGH effort with the highest risk that reviewers still judge it genre-mismatched. The dossier's own candid verdict is that the topic is in scope but the method and contribution type are largely out of scope; it lands last.

## 4. What to change for the top pick (AI & Society)

Concrete checklist to convert the current Public Choice manuscript to AI & Society style (effort: MEDIUM — mechanics are light, the real work is two new sections and a register shift; **no new data needed**):

**Substantive / framing (the load-bearing edits):**
- [ ] **Add a Theory / Conceptual-Framing section.** Engage gendered-language and algorithmic-fairness scholarship; open by problematizing the "alignment makes models fairer / AI removes bias" narrative — the "overcorrection" finding is the natural hook.
- [ ] **Add a Discussion + Implications section** (societal, policy, design implications). The current paper jumps Results → Conclusion; AS values an explicit Discussion for empirical work.
- [ ] **Reframe the unit of analysis** from econometric outcome variable to *sociotechnical phenomenon with ethical/policy stakes* — lead with the argument (aligned chat models overcorrect toward pro-female association, β ≈ −0.61, while occupational stereotypes persist) and present the regressions as evidence *in* that argument, not as the argument itself.
- [ ] **Co-bill a named contribution** beyond the finding — e.g. position the prompt-based audit pipeline / 896-check reproducibility protocol as a reusable framework (cf. AS Exemplar 1).
- [ ] **Translate the econometrics for a non-economist reader.** Keep the OLS LPM, robust SEs, and percentage-point interpretation, but narrate every headline coefficient in plain language; move the densest of the 20+ appendix regressions into anonymized supplementary material.

**Mechanical / format:**
- [ ] **Re-platform** to Word or the Springer Nature LaTeX class `sn-jnl.cls`; single-column, double-spaced review copy; decimal heading numbering, ≤3 levels, never skipping a level.
- [ ] **Restyle citations** to Springer "Basic" author-date: `(Author Year)` with **no comma**, author initials with **no periods**, `Volume:pages` joined by a colon, DOIs as full `https://doi.org/...` URLs.
- [ ] **Collapse the abstract** to a single unstructured paragraph, ≤250 words (up to 450 if justified), **no citations**.
- [ ] **Expand main text** toward ~10,000 words / ~20 typeset pages (there is headroom).
- [ ] **Keywords:** keep 4–6 (current 5 already complies).
- [ ] **Figures/tables:** caption placement already matches (figures below, tables above) — leave as is; favor a few interpretive figures over many dense regression tables in the main text.

**Production gates (easily-missed, can return the manuscript as incomplete):**
- [ ] **Anonymize fully** for double-blind review (strip author names/affiliations/identifying info from text, figures, and supplementary files) and supply a **separate Title Page** with title, authors, affiliations, corresponding-author contact, plus acknowledgements/funding.
- [ ] **Add the "Statements and Declarations" block:** Competing Interests, Author Contributions, Funding, **AI-use disclosure** (document the GPT-model usage in the Methods section), and a data-availability statement. ("Submissions that do not include relevant declarations will be returned as incomplete.")
