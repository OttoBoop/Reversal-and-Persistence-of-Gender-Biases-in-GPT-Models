# Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research (JAIR) -- Style & Fit Dossier

## 1. Snapshot

The **Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research (JAIR)** is published by the **AI Access Foundation**, a non-profit corporation established specifically to operate the journal on an open-access basis. JAIR is one of the oldest electronic, peer-reviewed scientific journals in the field, founded in 1993, and it occupies an unusual position in the scholarly-publishing landscape: it is a **diamond/platinum open-access** venue. As its submissions page states, "JAIR does not charge authors for submitting or publishing articles," and every article is released under a **Creative Commons CC BY** license. There is no article-processing charge (APC) and no subscription barrier on either side. Articles are "published on the internet immediately upon receiving the final versions," giving the journal a rolling-publication character rather than discrete printed issues. Sources: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about/submissions and https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about.

The **aims & scope** are deliberately broad within a single discipline: JAIR covers "all areas of artificial intelligence," explicitly enumerating agents and multi-agent systems, automated reasoning, constraint processing and search, knowledge representation, machine learning, natural language processing, planning and scheduling, robotics and computer vision, uncertainty in AI, and -- in more recent additions reflecting the field's evolution -- generative AI, human-AI interaction, and the ethics of AI. The disciplinary **center of gravity is computer science / artificial intelligence in the technical sense**. This is not an interdisciplinary social-science venue, nor an HCI/qualitative venue, nor an economics or political-economy venue. It is a core AI journal that happens to have widened its scope to include ethics and generative AI as legitimate AI subfields. Source: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about.

The **article types** JAIR publishes are: **refereed research articles** (the bulk of the journal), **survey/review articles** (the journal is "committed to publishing high-quality survey articles of broad interest"), and **technical notes** (concise presentations of a result or methodology). The empirical/conceptual/qualitative/review mix skews heavily toward **technical-methodological and theoretical** contributions, with **comprehensive surveys** as a distinctively valued second category. New methods, algorithms, formal results (theorems and proofs), and large benchmark experiments are all squarely in-house. Purely **qualitative** social-science work, and **conceptual** work in the humanities sense, are atypical. The journal also runs **Special Tracks** alongside the main track -- notably a **"Fairness and Bias in AI" Special Track** (seeded from the AEQUITAS workshop at ECAI 2023) -- and authors are required to select the correct Section/track at submission because, in the journal's words, it is "essential to identify the right set of reviewers." Sources: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about and https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/SpecialTrack-FBAI.

The **typical audience** is AI researchers and practitioners -- people who read and write technical AI papers, attend AAAI/IJCAI/NeurIPS/ACL-class venues, and expect formal definitions, algorithms, benchmark experiments, or rigorous surveys. The reader of a JAIR article expects a long, comprehensive, technically self-contained treatment, not a short empirical note.

## 2. Style guide / author guidelines

**Template / format.** LaTeX is the expected and explicitly requested format. JAIR maintains **its own LaTeX class/style** rather than adopting a third-party class such as Springer's `sn-jnl`, ACM's `acmart`, or IEEE's templates. The historical authors' guide lists LaTeX, FrameMaker, and GROFF as accepted, but states that "**LaTeX is requested if possible**." The document-class invocation is `\documentclass[jair,11pt]{article}` (a real published sample uses `\documentclass[jair,11pt,letterpaper]{article}`), with `\usepackage{jair}` and `\usepackage{theapa}`, and the bibliography set via `\bibliographystyle{theapa}`. The required style files -- `jair.sty` (LaTeX style) and the `theapa` package, comprising `theapa.sty` and `theapa.bst` -- are distributed in the **JAIR Author Kit**, and an official read-only **Overleaf template** exists at https://www.overleaf.com/read/hycbzkdksrzz ("click Menu then Copy Project" to start). Layout is **11-point on US letterpaper**, with a **single-column body** (standard `article`-class text block; high confidence from the published two-up PDFs, though the exact `jair.sty` column directive is **UNCONFIRMED** from a primary fetch). Sources: http://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/project/jair/pub/information/format/authors-guide.txt ; https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/authorinstrs ; https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/formatting.

**Citation style.** This is one of JAIR's most distinctive and load-bearing house features: **author-date (author-year), via the APA-flavored "theapa" style. It is NOT numeric and NOT ACM Reference Format.** The `theapa.bst` file "implements the APA (author-year) citation format." The in-text commands and their exact rendered forms are:

- `\cite{...}` -> parenthetical, e.g. `\cite[p.~32]{Apt88}` renders as "(e.g. see Apt, 1988, p. 32)";
- `\citeA{...}` -> textual/narrative variant (author woven into the sentence);
- `\citeauthor{...}` -> author surname only (e.g. "Altman");
- `\citeyear{...}` -> year in parentheses (e.g. "(1968)");
- `\shortcite / \shortciteA / \shortciteauthor` -> force the short ("et al.") form; `\fullcite / \fullciteA / \fullciteauthor` -> force the full author-list form.

The style performs **automatic et-al. switching**: with three or more authors, the first citation prints "(Aha, Kibler, & Albert, 1991)" and subsequent citations collapse to "(Aha et al., 1991)." The **reference list** is alphabetical by author surname, per APA convention, and is formatted by `theapa.bst`. The named style, in plain terms, is **APA author-date**. One caveat for reproducibility-minded authors: the stock JAIR `theapa.bst` is old ("last updated more than 25 years ago") and does **not natively print DOI/URL** fields; a community drop-in, `newtheapa.bst`, adds DOI/URL support and can be invoked via `\bibliographystyle{newtheapa}`. DOIs are therefore **not mandated nor auto-rendered** by the official style. Sources: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/project/jair/pub/information/format/latex/theapa.txt ; https://github.com/eladden/JAIR_theapa_bst_with_doi_url.

**Abstract rules.** JAIR **encourages but does not mandate structured abstracts**; the journal "introduced structured abstracts standardizing objectives, methods, and findings to improve reader accessibility." The author instructions add the guidance that "**your abstract should read well, as it will be used to advertise your paper.**" In practice (see Section 3), the de-facto norm is a **single-paragraph narrative abstract**, not the headed Objective/Methods/Findings form. The **exact word limit is UNCONFIRMED** -- no specific number surfaced from a primary fetch -- and a "no citations in the abstract" rule, while standard practice and observed in the exemplars, could **not be confirmed as an explicit JAIR rule** and is flagged **UNCONFIRMED**. Sources: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about/submissions ; https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/authorinstrs.

**Structure expectations.** JAIR imposes **no rigid IMRaD template**. Because it publishes theory/algorithm papers, empirical/ML papers, and surveys side by side, structure follows the work: formal results with theorems and proofs, large experimental sections, and survey taxonomies all appear. **IMRaD is not required. Explicit numbered hypotheses (H1/H2) are not required and are uncommon** outside formal empirical-study papers. A dedicated social-science-style "theory/conceptual development" section and a separate "Discussion + Implications" section are **not mandated** -- these are discipline norms elsewhere, not JAIR house rules. What JAIR *does* prescribe are concrete **heading conventions** (CONFIRMED house rules): "**Capitalize section and subsection titles**"; "**Do not start a section with a subsection**" (each section must open with body text before any subsection); "**Capitalize references to figures and tables**," e.g. "In Figure 7..."; **appendices come after the Acknowledgments and are lettered** ("Appendix A," "Appendix B"); and **running heads** show authors' names on even pages and the paper title on odd pages from page 3. Sources: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about ; https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/authorinstrs.

**Figures and tables.** Caption placement is **BELOW the illustration** -- "The figure or table number and the caption should appear **under** the illustration." This is a notable house rule because it applies to *tables as well as figures* (many venues place table captions above). Figures and tables are numbered sequentially and referenced with capitalized "Figure"/"Table." There is an explicit minimum-text-size rule: "Captions, labels, and other text in illustrations must be **at least nine-point type**." Color is permitted (online open-access PDF). Specific resolution/DPI requirements and any "source note" convention were **not surfaced as explicit JAIR rules** and are **UNCONFIRMED**. Source: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/authorinstrs.

**Keywords.** Keywords/indexing terms are requested for searchability, but the **exact required count/range and format are UNCONFIRMED** from a primary fetch. Source: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about/submissions.

**Length norms.** **No hard page or word limit surfaced.** JAIR is known for long, comprehensive articles -- published volumes routinely run 40-100+ pages, and the exemplars below are 29-40 pages. Treat "no fixed page limit" as the working norm, but the exact policy is **UNCONFIRMED**. The journal **strongly encourages online appendices**: articles "may be accompanied by online appendices containing data, demonstrations, instructions for obtaining source code, or the source code itself," and "JAIR strongly encourages authors to include such appendices." These online appendices (distinct from the lettered in-text appendices) are JAIR's primary **reproducibility/supplementary mechanism**. Sources: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about/submissions ; http://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/project/jair/pub/information/format/authors-guide.txt.

**Required statements / declarations.**
- **AI-use disclosure / responsibility (CONFIRMED):** "Authors are fully responsible for the originality, accuracy, and integrity of all submitted content. While artificial intelligence tools, including large language models, may be used to assist... such as editing, refining language, generating ideas, or analyzing data -- the **core contributions of a paper must come from the authors**." AI cannot be listed as an author (aligns with COPE). Source: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about/submissions.
- **Copyright / CC BY notice (CONFIRMED, required in every paper):** "each paper must include a copyright notice referencing the Creative Commons CC BY license." Source: as above.
- **Originality / attribution (CONFIRMED):** submissions must meet the highest originality and significance standards and "clearly acknowledge the contributions of their predecessors."
- **Conflict of interest, funding, data-availability, author-contribution, ethics/IRB statements:** No JAIR-specific *mandatory templated* statements were confirmed on a primary jair.org page. JAIR's reproducibility expectation is met chiefly through **online appendices / source-code availability** rather than a formal "Data Availability Statement." Treat formal COI/funding/data/ethics statement requirements as **UNCONFIRMED / not explicitly mandated** by JAIR house style.
- **Positionality / reflexivity:** Not applicable and **not required** -- JAIR is a technical AI venue, not an HCI/qualitative venue. (Almost certainly none exists; flagged UNCONFIRMED only in the trivial sense that the absence of a rule cannot be quoted.)

**Review model.** JAIR review is **single-blind** in the standard track -- reviewers are anonymous to authors, but **authors are NOT required to anonymize** their submissions. There is no double-blind requirement in the standard track, so full author-identity blinding should be treated as **not required** (UNCONFIRMED as a requirement). Source: https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/about/submissions.

**Headline signal (decisive for the comparison).** **JAIR essentially does NOT publish GPT/ChatGPT gender-bias audit papers of the kind the user's paper is.** Repeated targeted searches for JAIR + "ChatGPT/GPT audit," "gender stereotype occupation," and "fairness audit" surfaced **zero** matching JAIR articles. That exact genre -- empirical prompt-based audits of commercial LLMs for gender bias -- lives in *AI & Society*, *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* (Nature), *PNAS Nexus*, *ScienceDirect / Computers in Human Behavior*, and on arXiv, not in JAIR. What JAIR *does* publish near this topic is **methodological, conceptual, formal-fairness, and survey** work. The journal even maintains a dedicated **"Fairness and Bias in AI" Special Track**, so the *topic area* is genuinely in scope -- but the house treatment is *measurement-theoretic / technical*, not *audit-of-a-product*. The three closest verifiable, recent, on-topic exemplars follow; all are diamond OA under CC BY.

**Exemplar 1 -- the best topical match (NLP + bias + measurement).** *Undesirable Biases in NLP: Addressing Challenges of Measurement*, by Oskar van der Wal, Dominik Bachmann, Alina Leidinger, Leendert van Maanen, Willem Zuidema, and Katrin Schulz (Amsterdam / Utrecht), **2024, JAIR Vol. 79, pp. 1-40**. DOI https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.15195 ; landing https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/15195 ; arXiv mirror https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13709. This is the single most on-point JAIR article for the user's project: it is explicitly about bias in NLP / LLMs, opening "As Large Language Models and Natural Language Processing technology rapidly develop... this technology has displayed harmful biases, from generating derogatory stereotypes to producing disparate outcomes for different social groups." But the framing is telling -- rather than auditing a model, it interrogates **how we measure** bias, importing **psychometrics** (construct validity and reliability). The **abstract** is a single-paragraph narrative (~180-200 words), no in-text citations, problem -> gap -> approach -> payoff. **Structure** (reconstructed): Introduction -> background on NLP bias measures (WEAT etc.) -> Construct Validity -> Reliability -> application to existing metrics -> recommendations -> conclusion -> acknowledgments -> references; IMRaD is *not* followed. **Citations** are author-date APA ("(Caliskan et al., 2017)" / "Caliskan et al. (2017)"), alphabetical reference list. **Tone** is interdisciplinary CS x measurement-theory, methodological-critique register. **Methods/results:** conceptual + methodological, illustrated with worked examples; **no new empirical audit, no regression, no theorems, no numbered hypotheses, no social-science theory section.** **Figures/tables** are sparse (exact count unverified). **Length** ~40 pages. **Contribution type:** conceptual/methodological synthesis.

**Exemplar 2 -- fairness + legality + mitigation (applied, regulatory).** *Bias Mitigation Methods: Applicability, Legality, and Recommendations for Development*, by Madeleine Waller, Odinaldo Rodrigues, Michelle Seng Ah Lee, and Oana Cocarascu (King's College London), **2024, JAIR Vol. 81, pp. 1043-1078**. DOI https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.16759 ; landing https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/16759 ; extended arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20020. A squarely fairness/bias paper exemplifying JAIR's **survey-plus-recommendations** article type with a real-world recruitment case study; it treats sex/gender as the protected attribute (explicitly noting the binary "male/female" simplification common in fairness work). **Abstract:** single-paragraph narrative (~150-180 words), no citations. **Structure** (reconstructed): Introduction -> definitions (fairness metrics, protected attributes, ADMS) -> taxonomy of mitigation methods (pre-/in-/post-processing) -> applicability analysis -> legality analysis across EU/UK/US law -> recruitment case study -> recommendations -> conclusion -> references. **Citations:** author-date theapa/APA. **Tone:** CS x law/policy, practitioner- and regulator-facing. **Methods/results:** mixed survey + conceptual/legal analysis anchored by one worked case study; **no theorems, no hypotheses, no regression.** **Tables:** taxonomy/comparison tables (table-heavy for JAIR; exact counts unverified). **Length** ~36 pages. **Contribution type:** survey + normative/legal argument + recommendations.

**Exemplar 3 -- formal/quantitative fairness method (the technical pole).** *Uncertainty as a Fairness Measure*, by Selim Kuzucu, Jiaee Cheong, Hatice Gunes, and Sinan Kalkan (Cambridge / METU), **2024, JAIR Vol. 81, pp. 307-335**. Landing https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/16041 ; PDF https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/16041/27089/41499. A fairness paper representative of JAIR's **core technical/ML-methods identity** -- it proposes *new* fairness measures grounded in predictive uncertainty (aleatoric vs. epistemic). Bias-adjacent but **not** about LLMs or gender. **Abstract:** single-paragraph narrative (~170-200 words), gap -> demonstration -> contribution, no citations. **Structure** (reconstructed): Introduction -> related work -> preliminaries/definitions (group vs. individual fairness; aleatoric vs. epistemic uncertainty) -> proposed measures (formal definitions, equations) -> experimental setup on benchmark datasets -> experiments & results -> discussion -> conclusion. **Citations:** author-date theapa/APA. **Tone:** mainstream technical ML/CS, claims-and-evidence register. **Methods/results:** quantitative + formal -- mathematical definitions followed by empirical benchmark experiments; **no user study, no regression-as-social-science, no numbered hypotheses** (a preliminaries/definitions block plays the role of a theory section). **Figures/tables:** figure- and table-rich (the most graphics-dense of the three; exact counts unverified). **Length** ~29 pages. **Contribution type:** new method + supporting empirical evaluation.

**Cross-paper synthesis.** All three abstracts are **single-paragraph narrative** (~150-200 words), no in-text citations, written to "advertise the paper" -- the headed structured form is *encouraged but unused* by these papers. Citations are uniformly **author-date / APA (theapa)**, never numeric, never ACM -- the single most distinctive surface feature versus the ACL/ACM/IEEE venues where LLM-bias work usually appears. **No IMRaD straitjacket:** a methodology-critique paper, a survey+legal paper, and a method+experiments paper coexist. **Length is long** (29-40 pp.). **No numbered hypotheses or social-science "Theory & Hypotheses" section** in any of them; where theory appears it is a "Preliminaries/Definitions" or "Background" block. The disciplinary center of gravity is **technical/methodological CS**, with measurement-theory and law/ethics as accepted flavors. **Product-audit empirical findings -- the user's genre -- are essentially absent from the journal.**

**Prose.** The deepest divergence is **disciplinary**, not cosmetic. The user's paper is an *economics* paper: it adopts the resume-audit tradition of Bertrand and Mullainathan, treats "P(male character)" as a dependent variable, estimates an OLS linear-probability model with heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors, and reads coefficients as percentage points. JAIR's readership does not think in those terms. At JAIR, "bias measurement" means construct validity and reliability (Exemplar 1), formal fairness definitions over protected groups (Exemplar 3), or a taxonomy of mitigation methods (Exemplar 2) -- it does not mean a regression table whose betas are interpreted as labor-market-style effects. So while the *topic* (LLM gender bias) is in scope thanks to the Fairness & Bias Special Track, the *method idiom* and the *contribution shape* are foreign to the venue.

The **surface-level** differences are comparatively easy: the citation style is already author-date, so the move is mechanical (from `apalike`/`citet`/`citep` to `theapa`/`\cite`/`\citeA`/`\citeyear`); the abstract needs only trimming to a single narrative paragraph; and the figure/table convention requires moving **table captions below** (the user currently places table captions above), keeping all in-artwork text at >=9pt, and capitalizing cross-references. The structure is partially compatible -- JAIR imposes no IMRaD and tolerates a Methodology-heavy, equation-in-text layout -- but the user's Test 1/Test 2/Test 3 results organization, absence of a Discussion section, and absence of numbered hypotheses are all *fine* at JAIR (none are required). The friction is not the section list; it is that an audit-shaped paper is not what JAIR readers expect, and the paper would be reviewed by AI researchers looking for a *method or measurement* contribution.

A subtle but real point: JAIR **expects length and comprehensiveness**. The user's ~15-page main text is short by JAIR standards (29-40+ pp. is normal). The user's large appendix, Jupyter notebook, CSVs, and 896 audit checks are actually a strong asset here, because JAIR *strongly encourages online appendices containing data and source code* as its reproducibility mechanism -- this part of the paper aligns unusually well with house norms.

## 5. Fit & recommendation

**Fit as-is: poor on genre, acceptable on surface, in-scope on topic.** The honest verdict is that the user's paper, *in its current economist's audit form*, is a weak fit for JAIR. The topic is legitimately in scope (the Fairness & Bias in AI Special Track exists, and Exemplar 1 is explicitly about bias in NLP/LLMs), and several surface conventions are already close (author-date citations, equation-in-text methodology, large reproducibility appendix). But the **genre** -- "we prompted GPT across many models and counted stereotypes, then ran OLS" -- has essentially **zero published precedent at JAIR**, and the **method idiom** (labor-economics regression with percentage-point coefficients) is not how JAIR's AI-researcher reviewers frame or evaluate bias work.

**Specific changes required.** (1) Adopt the **JAIR LaTeX class** (`\documentclass[jair,11pt,letterpaper]`, `jair.sty`, `theapa`) via the Overleaf Author Kit. (2) Convert citations to **theapa** commands (mechanical, since already author-date). (3) Trim the abstract to a **single narrative paragraph** (consider, but do not require, the encouraged Objective/Methods/Findings structure). (4) Move **table captions below** the tables; ensure all in-artwork text is **>=9pt**; capitalize "Figure N"/"Table N." (5) Add the **CC BY copyright notice** and an **AI-use statement** acknowledging that core contributions are the authors'. (6) Make appendices **lettered and placed after Acknowledgments**, and route data/code/notebooks into JAIR-style **online appendices**. (7) Most importantly, **reframe the contribution** (below). (8) Likely **expand** toward a more comprehensive, longer treatment.

**Adaptation effort rating: HIGH.** Justification: the surface edits (citations, captions, template, abstract, statements) are individually Low-effort, but the dominant cost is the **conceptual reframing and re-positioning** required to make an economist's product-audit read as a JAIR-appropriate AI contribution. That is not a formatting task -- it is a rewrite of the paper's intellectual frame, its method presentation, and very likely its scope/length. Reframing an OLS-driven audit into a measurement-methodology, formal-fairness, or survey-with-recommendations contribution is substantial work, and without it the paper is genre-mismatched to the venue. Hence High, not Medium.

**The single most important reframing the journal would demand:** stop presenting the paper as an *empirical audit that measures a quantity of bias*, and present it instead as a **methodological / measurement contribution** -- e.g., "a reproducible, multi-model *methodology* for quantifying gender-association drift across LLM generations, with a measurement framework and a longitudinal benchmark," foregrounding the *method and the cross-model measurement design* (the 14+ models, 62k+ generations, 896 audit checks) as the contribution, with the "overcorrection" finding as a demonstration of the method rather than the headline result. The OLS machinery should be recast as one instrument within a measurement framework, not as the labor-economics centerpiece. Alternatively, the paper could be rebuilt as a **comprehensive survey-with-recommendations** of LLM gender-bias measurement (a valued JAIR type), or as a **formal/quantitative fairness-measure** contribution (Exemplar 3 style). Absent one of these reframings, the candid recommendation is that the paper's topic is in scope but its **method and contribution type are largely out of scope for JAIR**, and a venue such as *AI & Society*, *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, or *PNAS Nexus* (where this exact audit genre is published) -- or the user's current Public Choice target -- remains a better natural home.
